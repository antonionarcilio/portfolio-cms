question_two: What value do I deliver that an AI alone cannot deliver?
response_two: |-
  **An identity:**

  Every day, creating an attractive and functional interface for a product — whether it's a web application, a website, a blog, or a landing page — is getting easier. That's thanks to the advancement of AI, which, with increasingly powerful tools, enables an ordinary user to bring their idea 'off the page' with a simple prompt.

  The problem is that an AI with no control, orchestration, or managed without any technical knowledge can lead to a generic result. It's very easy to find on the web today products that look alike, infamous products with an 'AI look'.

  That's where a professional with experience, technical knowledge, and a focus on quality, usability, and performance steps in to change this scenario.

  An identity is not just in the appearance of a product, but in the ability to give it its own characteristics, make it recognizable, and make sense within what is being built. It's what makes a product stop being just another visually pleasing solution and start having a presence of its own.

  **A criterion:**

  The easier it becomes to produce, the greater the number of possibilities becomes. And, faced with so many possibilities, knowing how to choose becomes as valuable as knowing how to build.

  An AI can present different solutions to the same problem, but knowledge is needed to evaluate what has been produced, understand its implications, and decide what is really worth using. Not everything that can be done needs to be done, and not everything that seems to work represents the best solution.

  **A solution:**

  There is also value in turning complexity into simplicity.

  A product can have countless features and possibilities, but, for whoever is on the other side, the experience needs to be clear and intuitive. Finding a simple way to solve a complex problem is as important as the implementation of the solution itself.

  **Quality:**

  Finally, a solution should not be evaluated only by the fact that it works or by the appearance it has.

  Quality is also in the way it is used, in its performance, and in the way its elements behave and relate to one another. These are details that can go unnoticed when the goal is merely to generate something functional, but they make a difference in the final result.

  In the end, it's not just about using a tool capable of producing interfaces. It's about knowing what to build, why to build it, and how to make it make sense for the product and for those who will use it.
